# Family-specific design questions

These are routes for choosing evidence and checks, not universal performance predictors.

- **Spinel oxides (AB2O4)**: specify normal/inverse or otherwise evidenced cation site distribution, oxidation states and defects. Separate tetrahedral and octahedral sites and their spin/coordination models. Test charge balance only from explicit species assignments. Do not use Goldschmidt's ABO3 rule as a generic rejection test. eg descriptor optima depend on the studied spinel family and reaction; the 2022 spinel POD study below reports a different optimum from the perovskite study.
- **Single-atom materials**: identify metal, coordination atoms/count, carrier, defects and axial ligands. Compare formation and aggregation alternatives on consistent chemical-potential and stoichiometric reference bases; do not compare unlike raw energies. d-band and adsorption scaling arguments need an applicable literature or computed model. Coordination and spin require structural evidence or explicit assumptions.
- **MOFs**: specify metal node, ligand and topology; inspect water/pH/temperature stability, substrate access, active-site accessibility and leaching. A reported composition does not determine a unique pore geometry or an unsaturated site count. Require appropriate structural or adsorption evidence before rejecting a candidate on pore fit.
- **Carbon materials**: specify dopant, location, concentration, defects and surface groups. A bulk composition does not determine a unique active site. Separate measured surface chemistry from a proposed atomistic site; validate using appropriate XPS/Raman/site and kinetic evidence.
- **Metal/alloy particles**: distinguish composition from size, facet, strain and ligand coverage. Inspect miscibility, segregation and ligand/medium compatibility using the relevant synthesis and reaction conditions. Do not assign a d-band center from a name alone.
- **Perovskite oxides (ABO3)**: specify site assignments, valence/oxygen stoichiometry and coordination. Goldschmidt-style radii arguments are geometry/formability heuristics, not a computed thermodynamic stability result. State the source of radii and their coordination/oxidation conventions. Apply eg/covalency descriptors only with the corresponding reaction and site model.
